Career Ops

Built 2026 · in daily use

A job search is mostly grind: reading hundreds of postings to find the few worth an afternoon, then losing track of where everything stands. This removes that. Nothing gets scanned by hand, and every question you actually have about a role is answered in one place before you decide.

It watches 87 companies' job boards and surfaces only what matches, ranked by how recently a role was posted, because posting age predicts a reply better than how well you match. For anything it surfaces it already knows the fit against a written profile, the published pay band, what employees say about working there, and whether you have applied before and what happened. Status is never typed in: it reads your mail and derives where each application stands, so the board is right without being maintained.

The interesting failures were all the same shape. The software would state something untrue with complete confidence, close a live application as rejected, and be believed because it looked authoritative. Most of the work is teaching it to say it isn't sure.

How the work actually went

Three write-ups of specific problems, what was tried, and what came of it. Longer than a bullet point and more honest about the middle part.

What I got wrong

For a long time I treated cross-functional process work as a design problem. It isn't, or not only. Building the mechanism is the easy half. The hard half is that a new operating cadence quietly redistributes who decides what, and if you don't work that part deliberately you end up with a technically correct system nobody adopts. I have shipped a few of those. Now I spend as much time on who has to agree as on how the thing works.

Interactive Campaign Dashboard Read

The Challenge

Managing 56 initiatives across 4 product areas and 200+ stakeholders requires more than spreadsheets. Leadership needed real-time visibility into performance metrics, but existing reporting was manual, inconsistent, and always a week behind.

"By the time we got the data, it was already too late to course-correct." — VP, Partnerships

The Approach

I designed and built an automated executive dashboard using Tableau connected to BigQuery, with the goal of providing real-time insights that actually drive decisions. The key principles:

  • Executive-first design: Lead with the 4-5 metrics that matter most, everything else is drill-down
  • Automated data pipeline: SQL scripts pull from 6 source systems nightly, with data validation checks
  • Action-oriented visualizations: Every chart answers a specific question leadership asks weekly
  • Mobile-responsive: Execs check dashboards on phones between meetings

Key Features

The dashboard includes several interconnected views:

  • KPI Summary: Revenue attainment, initiative progress, stakeholder alignment, and CSAT at a glance
  • Vertical Performance: Target vs. actual revenue by business vertical with variance highlighting
  • Initiative Tracker: Gantt-style view of all active initiatives with health indicators
  • Risk Register: Automated flagging of at-risk initiatives based on milestone slippage

Technical Implementation

The backend leverages BigQuery for data warehousing with scheduled SQL queries that transform raw data into analysis-ready tables. Key technical decisions included:

  • Incremental loading patterns to minimize query costs
  • Parameterized date filters for historical comparison
  • Row-level security for team-specific views
  • Slack integration for automated weekly summaries

Impact

The dashboard transformed how leadership engages with program data. Weekly governance meetings now start with a shared view of reality, and decisions are made with confidence rather than gut feel.

The automated reporting eliminated 40+ hours of manual work monthly and reduced "data reconciliation" debates in leadership meetings by an estimated 80%.

Streamlining Partner Onboarding Read

The Problem

Partner onboarding was taking an average of 47 days from signed contract to first revenue. For a business where time-to-revenue directly impacts quarterly targets, this was unacceptable. Worse, the process was inconsistent—some partners onboarded in 3 weeks, others took 3 months.

The Approach

I led a cross-functional process improvement initiative using Lean Six Sigma methodology. The project followed DMAIC:

  • Define: Mapped the current state with all 6 teams, identified the critical path
  • Measure: Instrumented each step to understand where time was actually spent
  • Analyze: Root cause analysis revealed 60% of delays came from just 3 handoff points
  • Improve: Redesigned the workflow, introduced parallel processing, created SLAs
  • Control: Built automated tracking dashboard, established weekly standup cadence
The biggest insight: most delays weren't from slow work—they were from work sitting in queues between teams. The solution wasn't to work faster, but to eliminate wait time.

Key Changes

The redesigned process included several structural improvements:

  • Parallel workstreams: Technical setup and legal review now happen simultaneously
  • Single point of contact: One onboarding lead owns the relationship end-to-end
  • Standardized templates: 80% of partners fit standard configurations, reducing custom work
  • Automated status updates: Partners see real-time progress, reducing check-in overhead

Building & Shipping My First App Read
Download Mirror on Google Play

The Idea

Mirror started as a personal experiment: could I build a tool that helps people reflect more meaningfully on their lives, using AI as a thoughtful companion rather than just a chatbot?

As someone who values mindfulness but struggles with consistency, I wanted to create something that would make reflection feel natural, not like another task on the to-do list.

The goal wasn't to build another journaling app—it was to create a space for genuine self-discovery, powered by AI that listens more than it talks.

The Product

Mirror is a spiritual-reflection tool with three core features:

  • Daily Prompts: Thoughtful questions tailored to your current life context, not generic inspirational quotes
  • Pattern Recognition: AI that identifies themes across your reflections over time, surfacing insights you might miss
  • Privacy-First Architecture: All data encrypted end-to-end; the AI learns your patterns without storing your actual entries

From Idea to Revenue

The app is now live on Google Play and generating revenue through Meta Network Ads. Shipping something from scratch taught me more about product development than years of managing other people's products.

What I Learned

Building Mirror taught me more about product development than any course could:

  • Start with the job-to-be-done: Users don't want an app—they want to feel more self-aware
  • Simplicity is hard: I cut 60% of my initial features to focus on what actually mattered
  • Shipping beats perfection: The version I launched wasn't the version I imagined, but it was real
  • Monetization validates value: People paying for your product is the ultimate product-market fit signal
