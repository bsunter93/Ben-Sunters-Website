## The three new reviewers (simulated personas modeled on public roles; not their words)

### Simulated persona modeled on the public role of Thomas Kurian; not his words. Rating 6.

I read the docs first, as I would with any data product: REVIEW_GUIDE, the context brief (sections 1–2, 7), `chain_check.py`, the CI workflow, the sources pass. Then I spent the time in the browser. My rating is 6 of 10, and the reason is that the methodology is more honest than the pipeline is reproducible.

What I respect. The checker is small, readable and deterministic: one file, 432 lines, every placebo window on a fixed grid (every 14th day of the page's own history), the dose-response permutation seeded (`random.Random(1466)`). The verdict vocabulary (measured / timed / reported / plausible / busted) is defined in code, repeated in the product's "How it works", and enforced in the UI: a measured card reads "a jump this big showed up at only 1.3% of placebo windows (p = 0.013)", and at the floor it says "no placebo window rose this much (p = 0.01)" rather than inventing precision. Busted claims are struck through with the dates that bust them (The Jungle: Feb 26 vs Feb 21, 1906). The page loaded in under 2 s, ran at 55 fps, and threw zero console errors across six pages. Nulls are shown as findings. That is the right culture.

Where it breaks as a platform. First, provenance is not first-class across routes. The flagship story, Tiger King, exists twice: the chain checker rates "Congress passes the Big Cat Act" as *reported*, but the pond a user sees is built from a separate map file plus a hand-coded `MAPS` table in the HTML that pins the Act to `lv:"plausible"`. Same slug, two ratings, and the one on screen is a literal in the page. Second, the results file carries only a run date: no commit SHA of the checker, no hash of inputs, no snapshot of the Wikipedia series beyond a 16-week window. Because the placebo pool grows with page history, a p-value computed in 2026 will not be the p-value computed in 2027, and nothing records which version a card showed. Third, CI runs only on pushes to `claude/**` branches, never on main and never on a schedule, and commits results back with `[skip ci]`; there is no golden run to diff against. Fourth, 90% of steps (442 of 492) are hand-typed records and `none` steps; only 50 are computed, and 17 are measured. The honest description is "a hand-built timeline with a statistical check on a tenth of it".

The citation scorer is where governance is thinnest. It is a regex rubric, which I prefer to an opaque model, but its calibration set (22 items) lives inside the scorer file, graded by the owner, and the round-2 blind key is not in the repo. It gave the Tiger King floor speech a perfect 4 even though the sentence opens "As just one example", which is the scorer's own definition of an illustration cue. That score is what turns a citation into "Lasting mark · Reported" on screen.

Would a newsroom buy it? As a research notebook with a beautiful front end, yes, for one desk. As a product, not until a result is addressable: chain + checker version + data snapshot + run id, and a re-run reproduces the card. Biggest single risk: a reader cites a "measured" link that a later run no longer supports, with no record that it ever did.

Findings: Tiger King rated twice (map literal vs checker); no checker SHA, input hash or series snapshot in results; CI never runs on main or on a schedule, no golden diff; the citation scorer's calibration set lives in the scorer file and "As just one example" scored as an argument; a "no data" verdict renders as Plausible "Happened"; the chooser tabs navigated (fixed the same hour); no link check in CI; within-chance series drawn on equal footing in the chart with no p; multiple-comparison note absent; the acts pond's chooser label says Lasting marks; the same-stone row is hard to reach; the preview inliner is not in the repo.

### Simulated persona modeled on the public role of Elon Musk; not his words. Rating 5.

I opened it cold, hit Space on the intro: nothing. Space only drives the play button, which is behind the dialog. Clicked "Throw a stone", watched the rock fly and the front spread. Pretty. Then "/" , typed sputnik, Enter: landed on the right chain in under two seconds. Arrows step the ripples in date order; Step mode stops the clock at each one. The keyboard path is good and I would keep all of it.

Now the physics. What is the pond actually encoding? Radius is log time since the stone, angle is an "industry slice" someone chose by hand, stroke weight is a grade someone assigned. That is two numbers and a label. The feed on the right carries the same two numbers plus the text, so the pond is a second rendering of the feed with worse resolution. On this machine the pond burns 170–620 ms of main-thread time per second while JS is 10–26 ms of that: the cost is seven feTurbulence/filter nodes rasterizing water texture, not the simulation. Turn the filters off and task time drops roughly 2× (620→350, 425→172 ms/s) and the screenshots are nearly identical. The water is decoration you pay for every frame, including after the story has ended (24 layouts/s while paused at "Replay").

The method is more honest than the headline. The document says it plainly: "The ordering rule falsifies, it never proves." Good. But look at the catalog: 540 steps, 350 are `record` and 17 (3.1%) are "measured", 13 of those are Wikipedia pageview spikes. "Measured" licenses exactly one sentence, and the card says it: "This shows attention, not what people did next." The placebo test asks whether this page's own history ever rose this much in a window of the same length. That is a decent null for "rare for this page". It is not a test of the link to the stone, and the placebo windows start every 14 days with 60–180 day lengths, so n_placebo = 124 is maybe 10–20 independent draws; the quoted p resolution is optimistic. The ordering rule busts 30 steps, but 13 of them are "Claim:" steps the author wrote to be busted (`must_precede`). Real busts: 17 of 540. The dose-response design is the one piece I would call science: diff-in-diff, permutation null, pre-trend gate, pre-registered, outcome swapped with an addendum when YRBS could not carry it, result reported as a null (p = 0.10). There is one such step in the whole product.

What would falsify "A volcano → the bicycle"? Nothing here can. Two of its four links are rated Plausible ("the link is unproven") and the title still draws the arrow. The subtitle "order, not proof of cause" is the disclaimer carrying the headline.

Delete the water shaders, the 250 KB of base64 fonts and the 140 KB acts JSON nobody opened. Keep the checker, the ordering rule, the card prose, the keyboard. Add one thing: a per-chain "weakest link" score (min grade along the path) printed next to the title, so the arrow cannot promise more than its worst step.
