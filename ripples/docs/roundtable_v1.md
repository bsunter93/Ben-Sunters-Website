# Ripple, round four: the roundtable (Oct 4, 2026)

A simulation. Twelve simulated testers who reviewed Ripple independently meet to argue. Three of them are simulated personas modeled on three executive roles (a cloud platform, an engineering-led founder, a consumer-growth lead); they are labeled as such, they are not those people's words, and nothing below is anyone's real statement. The other nine are fictional testers from rounds one to three. The moderator is the Founder persona, at the owner's request.

**In the room:** MODERATOR (simulated persona: an engineering-led founder), CLOUD-EXEC PERSONA (simulated persona: a cloud-platform executive), GROWTH-EXEC PERSONA (simulated persona: a consumer-growth executive), Aisha (data journalist, laptop), Tom (retired machinist, iPad), Lena (student, iPhone SE), Marcus (warehouse worker, Android), Yuki (history teacher, projector), Earl (dispatcher, colorblind, laptop), Dani (16, phone), Priya (product manager, laptop), Walter (retired internist, tablet).

## What the room could not agree on

Whether the pond is the product or the index to it. Earl, Walter and Priya now read it as a chart and want it to carry more (dates, surprise, the spike); Lena, Marcus and Dani treat it as a tap pad under a caption and want it to carry less; the moderator wants it to look like the 3 percent that is measured, and Yuki and Tom think the 97 percent of careful, cited, unmeasured history is the reason to trust the 3. The arrow split the same way: everyone agreed one arrow should not mean five grades, and nobody agreed whether the fix is a smaller promise in the title or a louder grade beside it. And the reproducibility argument did not close: the Cloud-exec persona holds that a shared grade without a run id is a liability, the Growth-exec persona holds that a loop without sharing is a notebook, and the only thing both accepted is that the truncation test should run before either of them is proven right.
