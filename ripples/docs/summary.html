Ripple Map

An interactive explorer that uncovers the hidden knock-on effects of big events, wherever they show up, and shows exactly how sure we are about each one.

Executive and technical summary · Knock⌃On · Benjamin J. Sunter · bensunter.com/ripples · engine 6.1, with 6.2 in progress

Working draft · 26 Sep 2026, 03:50 UTC · yellow boxes are open numbers

Part I · Executive summary

1 · The vision

"The entire point of the app is uncovering hidden impacts from upstream events, regardless of where that impact shows up. Attribution and traceability with statistical rigor are key."

"I'd rather this be a really cool interactive entertainment app than a p2p collaborative or competitive game." · "Balance rigor with product usability."

The owner, 25–26 Sep 2026. Everything below is judged against these three lines.

In one sentence: Ripple Map takes an upstream shock (a hurricane, a heat wave, a model release, a rate decision), follows it across domains (jobless claims, grid demand, air travel, downloads, filings, prediction markets), and labels every step with a confidence tier that was earned by pre-registered tests, not asserted.

A hurricane leads to disaster declarations, then to jobless claims, then to new business filings. Each step on the map carries one of three words:

TierWhat it takesHow it reads
MeasuredPasses every test, including placebo and decoy controls and agreement from a second, independent forecast model"Consistent with a ripple from …; measured movement, not proof of cause"
LikelyStrong evidence, published with the measured rate at which findings like it turn out to be flukes"About 1 in K findings at this level are flukes"
WatchingPre-registered; its window is still open"The mechanism exists; no measurable move yet"

The site never claims proof of cause. It is free, has no accounts, and is not an investment tool.

2 · What makes it different, and hard to copy

Tests are locked before the data is read. Every candidate link, its weight and its look schedule are frozen and written to an append-only, hash-chained ledger before any post-event number is used. The chain verifies after every change (696 rows, 54 freeze batches, 0 tests without a frozen candidate, as of 03:30 UTC).

Every link is tested against fakes. Three placebo families (fake dates, other events, look-alike series) plus daily decoy events run through identical code. Any tier a decoy earns is false by construction, so the fluke rate is measured, not assumed.

A second opinion must agree. A nightly BigQuery forecast check (AI.CAUSAL_EFFECT) must agree before anything is called Measured. On its first calibrated run it flagged 0 of 13 eligible decoys.

Patterns across many past events (engine 6.2). Pooling past events of the same kind, and comparing hit regions with untouched ones, turns single noisy hops into findings like "across N past hurricanes, jobless claims in hit states rose X %", each with a measured fluke rate. This is what lets the product be both rigorous and full of interesting findings.

The failures stay on screen. Stops that stayed flat, retractions, the decoy "control ripple" and failed launch gates are published. Nobody else publishes the denominator.

3 · Rigor and usability, balanced

Strict tests favour obvious effects, and a map with three Measured hops is dull. The answer is not to loosen Measured. It is to add a second, plentiful kind of finding that is honestly labelled:

  • Measured stays as strict as engine 6.1. Engine 6.2 can only lift a statistical failure when a pre-registered regional contrast passes its own placebo test, and it adds a failure when a large family of past events disagrees.
  • Patterns and Likely findings are the everyday product. Each carries a strength word tied to a published fluke rate ("strong pattern", "pattern", "hint").
  • Watching is part of the show. Open windows, due dates and a reconstructed archive of past events give the map something to explore on day one.

4 · The experience

An interactive entertainment explorer: the hero is the Ripple Map of today's shocks, each growing a cascade that crosses domains over days and weeks. Tapping a step opens an evidence card (the mechanism, the series against its own seasonal normal, the lag, the fluke rate, how many independent sources agree, and the tier). Pattern cards summarise what happened across past events of the same kind. Shareable cascade cards are pre-rendered. A light "call the next ripple" moment is optional.

6 · Honest risks

  1. Rigor versus "hidden". Strict tests favour obvious effects, and so far no hop of any kind has reached Measured. Engine 6.2 is the answer; whether it yields enough interesting patterns is {{TBD: 6.2 result, n patterns with published fluke rate}}.
  2. Power is low on some channels. Before 6.1, a +5σ planted effect was caught at most 29 % of the time (physical series) and almost never on builder series. The 6.1 curve is {{TBD: 6.1 spike-in recall at +3σ by channel}}. The site never claims "no effect" beyond that curve.
  3. Live events start quiet. Effects take days to weeks. The archive and pattern findings fill the gap.
  4. Outcomes are mostly US data. Claims stay inside the measured geography.
  5. Free infrastructure. 377 of 500 MB used, the scheduler stalls during long statements, and free projects pause after a week idle. Supabase Pro (about $25/month) is recommended at launch.
  6. Design is not settled, and one operator runs everything.

Part II · Technical summary

9 · Architecture

Postgres is the engine; everything the public sees is a projection. Supabase edge functions collect daily aggregates into Postgres. The engine runs as SQL under pg_cron and writes an append-only, hash-chained ledger. GitHub Actions run the BigQuery work keylessly. The publisher freezes versioned JSON and share cards, and the site is a static GitHub Pages frontend. No visitor request ever reaches a source.

Demarcation: the lines we don't cross

Creative means more independent, legitimate signals, not grey access. Each source is graded by a written test (official channel? barrier? robots.txt? terms? personal data? published rate limit?), and the answer is recorded.

  • Honest identity. A declared User-Agent with a contact URL. The Wikimedia contact page is live at bensunter.com/ripples/methods/; every Wikimedia collector stops if it cannot be reached.
  • Stop, don't retry. A 429 or 503 stops the host until the next UTC midnight; a 401 or 403 stops it until the owner clears it. No CAPTCHA solving, proxies, IP or key rotation, or logged-in scraping.
  • Aggregates only. Counts, ranks and our own z-scores; no post text, handles or article bodies.
  • YouTube retention. No per-video row is ever written. The only raw cache is today's and yesterday's chart snapshot (always under 48 hours old); a daily job deletes any raw row older than 28 days as a safety net, inside YouTube's 30-day cached-data rule. Titles are read in memory only.
  • Documented exceptions. Wikimedia's API and SEC EDGAR full-text search (2 req/s, 200/day) skip the robots.txt check; every other rule applies.

11 · The cascade engine

A typed mechanism graph decides what to test. An event study with placebo controls decides whether the downstream series moved. Pooling across past events decides how much to trust a class of link. Engine versions: 6.0 (built), 6.1 (independent audit fixed), 6.2 (patterns and regional contrasts, in progress).

6.0: the core

  • Shocks and decoys. The day's real shocks each get a family (hazard, tech, policy, media, person). Matched decoy events, calm over the previous 14 days, go through identical generation, tests and chaining.
  • Candidates, frozen first. Family mappers (storm → state → claims, grid demand, travel, declarations), the mechanism library and Wikidata propose at most 60 paths per event. Paths, weights and look dates are hashed into the ledger before any post-onset number is read.
  • Baselines. Each series is compared only with itself: logged counts, shrunk weekday effects, a year-ago term, common-shock demeaning across a source's panel.
ARt = (x̃t − ŷt) / σ − ct  ·  σ = 1.4826·MAD  ·  ct = the day's median z across the panel
  • Three placebo families. Date (fake onsets of the same pair, season-matched), link (other events paired with the same series), topic (look-alike series at the real onset). A hop's p is the maximum across families; fewer than two families caps it at Likely.
  • Multiple testing. One weighted Benjamini–Hochberg run per day covers real and decoy hops together, with weights frozen before data.
  • Tiers. Measured needs q ≤ 0.05 at the final pre-registered look, an outcome channel, two independent channels, correct lag order, no common-shock day, no pre-trend, and the forecast gate. Attention-only hops are capped at Likely. Likely shows its one failing condition by name.
  • Chaining. Only Likely-or-better hops get children; a fork test draws a direct path as a sibling; nothing is multiplied along a chain for display.

6.1: the independent audit and its fixes

An independent statistician found the engine safe by absence of power (nothing had ever reached Measured) but with four anti-conservative holes that would matter once power was restored, plus a deploy gate that could not pass. All five blockers are fixed and deployed; the ledger still verifies bit-identically.

6.2: patterns across events, and hit versus untouched regions

The aim is many interesting, honestly labelled findings without loosening Measured.

  • Family pooling. Each frozen (event family × outcome) pair pools every past event of that kind. Per-event effects are standardised against 40 season-matched fake onsets, combined by random-effects meta-analysis, and judged by a placebo pool (300 replicates, each pooling one fake onset per real event), not by a normal approximation. Dose–response against event magnitude is reported as exploratory. The batch is one Benjamini–Hochberg family.
  • Regional contrasts. Difference-in-differences of hit regions against every untouched region (state claims, business applications, grid regions, declarations, weather), with a three-lead pre-trend test and 30 random in-space placebo groups; synthetic control with a rank test against 20 placebo groups where flagged. Holidays, national news and nationwide weather cancel; a shock that also hits the comparison regions does not.
  • New events borrow strength honestly. A live event's hop shows an empirical-Bayes shrunken estimate next to its own evidence and the family's, with a fixed note that the family result is not evidence about this event.
  • Tiering hook. A passing regional contrast can lift only statistical failures (q, placebo family, single channel, final look not reached); it never lifts a common-shock day, pre-trend, rival, reversal, parent or young-series failure. A family of 8+ past events that disagrees adds a Measured failure.

12 · Second opinion: the forecast gate

What it is. BigQuery's preview AI.CAUSAL_EFFECT fits a univariate forecast for one intervention date and returns the effect with an interval. What it is not. No discovery, no common-shock control, no multiple-testing control, so it cannot replace the engine. It is used only as an independent check.

  • Measured requires agreement: matching sign and an interval that excludes zero at the pre-registered horizon. The gate is wired into what the site publishes: a demoted hop shows the gate's reason ("forecast check pending", "disagrees", "cannot assess this series").
  • Eligibility rule after the first run. The first run called 51 of 81 decoys "significant", almost all from zero-heavy declaration counts (the forecast interval collapses to zero) and 91-day horizons fitted on too little history. A series now counts only if its interval has positive width and the horizon is at most 29 days; a hop with no eligible series cannot be Measured.
  • Wording: "vs forecast (range)", never "probability of causal effect". Cost guard: 204.8 GiB/day quota cap, $10 budget alert, partition pruning, dry-run bytes logged.

  • Publishing. Daily frozen cascade versions with payload hashes, mirrored JSON in Storage, pre-rendered share cards, RSS and .ics feeds, open CSV (CC BY, GREEN-derived fields only). Shared links point to frozen versions.
  • Contract tests. rm_contract_test() checks every public payload shape and the Measured wording; the forecast gate has its own tests. Engine unit tests T1–T25 cover freeze identity, placebo code paths, holidays, frozen look dates and the 6.2 hook (which may lift only the allowed failures). Latest full pass: {{TBD: engine test harness T1–T25 pass record}}.
  • Database. Row-level security on every table. Public RPCs are read-only, SECURITY DEFINER, empty search path, IDs-only inputs. Final advisor run: {{TBD: Supabase advisor ERROR / WARN counts}}.
  • Secrets. API keys live in Vault and GitHub secrets, never in the repo, logs or payloads; scoped credentials go only to their own hosts. Google Cloud uses Workload Identity Federation restricted to this repository, with BigQuery job-user and data-viewer roles only.
  • Visitors. No accounts, no tracking pixels; a hashed client ID with a daily salt.
  • Integrity. Tamper-evident, not tamper-proof: the chain head is not yet anchored outside the database.

15 · Honesty guardrails

We can claim

  • Timing: the series moved after the shock, with the lag
  • Size against the series' own normal, shrunk, with an interval
  • Rarity against calibrated placebos and decoys
  • How many independent channels agree
  • Patterns across N past events, with a measured fluke rate
  • Hit regions versus untouched regions

We never claim

  • "Caused", "drove", "because of"
  • A share of an effect, dollar or job amounts
  • Any probability for a whole chain
  • "No effect" beyond the published power curve
  • That a family pattern proves this event's effect
  • Tickers, trades or price forecasts

Numbers come only from SQL. Reconstructed archive lines are labelled on every surface and excluded from live denominators. Hazards with casualties get neutral copy and no teaser card.
