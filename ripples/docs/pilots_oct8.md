# Three outcome-first pilots (Oct 8, 2026)

Each pilot registered its plan and bars before pulling any outcome data. None passed. The studies pilot of the same
night did pass and ships (`docs/studies_v1.md`).

## Dog breeds

**Question:** do films, shows and memes move the breeds people register? NYC dog licenses (819,323 rows) and the UK
Kennel Club's 10-year tables (226 breeds, 2016 to 2025). A rise counts when a breed ranks in the top 2% of its year
against its own history.

| Bar | Needed | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Known checks graded Measured | 2 | 1 of 7 (Bluey and Australian Cattle Dogs, UK, 2021) | failed |
| New top 2% rises with a dated stone | 3 | 5 (Pomsky, Alaskan Klee Kai, Canaan Dog, Wire Fox Terrier, UK Bloodhound) | met on count; weak |
| Decoys passing | under 10% | 0 of 8 testable | met |

**Result:** did not pass. One Measured result out of 7 tests is about what chance gives (NYC chance rate 1.95%, UK
3.6%). The most interesting find is the Jindo, a Korean breed: 13 NYC dogs born in 2013, 236 born in 2017, the largest
rise of any breed in 2015, 2016 and 2017. It coincides with rescue flights from South Korean dog meat farms from
01/2015. By the ordering rule it grades busted, because the rise began with dogs born in 2014.

**Lesson:** the rank is not stable across counting units. Counting license rows in place of distinct dogs changes the
top list, and scaling by a breed's own history misses slow climbs. Rescue birth years are estimates, so the ordering
rule fits that mechanism poorly.

## Words

**Question:** did a book, film or event coin or launch a word? 548 (word, work) pairs from Wiktionary etymologies,
tested on Google Books Ngram: the word's frequency must break upward right after the stated year, more than at 20 other
years and more than 19 of 20 similar words.

| Bar | Needed | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Engine-found candidates graded Measured | 8 | 190 of 548 tested | met |
| Decoys passing | at most 3 of 40 (under 10%) | 10 of 38 tested (26%) | failed |

**Result:** did not pass. Random decoys behaved (0 of 18). Folk decoys did not (10 of 20).

**Lesson:** every folk decoy that passed pins a false origin story to the moment the phrase took off (iron curtain and
Churchill 1946, glass ceiling and the 1986 Wall Street Journal piece). The test shows that a word took off when the
stone appeared. The link to the stone itself rests on the dictionary etymology, so a Measured word ripple needs a
record of the coinage too. Merriam-Webster returned 403, so no word earned On the record.

## Degrees

**Question:** do shows move the majors students finish? IPEDS completions by field, 1991 to 2025. A rise counts when a
field ranks in the top 2% of its year and holds.

| Bar | Needed | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| CSI and forensic science in the top 2% in some year from 2003 to 2006 | yes | yes (2003 rank 6 of 373, 2005 rank 3 of 381) | met, busted on timing |
| New episodes graded Measured and On the record | 3 | 0 | failed |
| Decoys passing | 0 of 10 | 0 of 10 | met; a weak test (about 73% by chance) |

**Result:** did not pass. Forensic science degrees were already climbing before CSI premiered on 10/06/2000: 56
(1999), 71 (2000), 103 (2001), 152 (2002). By the ordering rule the CSI link is busted, and it stays On the record.

**Lesson:** the scan finds policy and labor markets. The largest lasting rises are reclassifications for immigration
rules (economics departments moving to the econometrics code for the STEM work extension) and a California transfer
degree law (78 to 100% of each large associate's degree rise came from California community colleges). Records were
the bottleneck: only one qualifying rise had a record naming a stone, and its timing fails.
