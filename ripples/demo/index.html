A show, a film, a disaster, a discovery. Its ripples spread out in time, each one rated for how sure we are, until something sticks: a law, an institution, a change in how people live.

Rings are time

The stone is the event. Each ring is time since it happened, so a ripple lands where it began. Each distinct ripple gets its own slice, labeled on the rim with the industry it reached.

Order, not a web

Every step must begin after the step before it. A step that started first is busted, however related it looks.

How sure

Measured: moved beyond normal in the data, after the step before. Timed: rose in the right order, not tested against chance. Reported: a credible source makes the link. Plausible: it happened; the link is unproven. Busted: wrong order, contradicted, or no evidence. Steps without an exact date sit at the middle of their likely range. Every dated step since 2015 also gets an attention check: did Wikipedia views of the thing it names rise at that date, tested against the page's own past? It rides along on the card; it does not change the step's rating.

Found by the engine

Ripple maps list things whose attention rose unusually after the event, found automatically among the Wikipedia articles that link to it. Attention is a lead, not a proven effect. The data: daily Wikipedia views, FRED and BLS series, Census retail sales, Zillow, Stack Overflow, US baby names and dated public records.

Ripple is an early build. Timing shows order, not proof of cause. Every test and every miss: ripple maps · discoveries.
