relevance_v1 (fixed rubric for C, catalyst relevance, and G, consequence significance)

Judge each candidate from its own fields only: the catalyst, the candidate outcome, the evidence line, and the existing
metadata in candidates.jsonl (mark kind, the study's place, reach and lasting fields, the chain's mark steps). Do not use
the panel ratings, the predictions, or the candidate's surprise. Record one C level and three G subscores, with a short
reason. C and G are separate: a link can be direct and minor, or loose and momentous.

C, catalyst relevance: how directly and substantively the evidence ties this outcome to this catalyst.
  1.00 direct        The evidence names the catalyst as the cause, the reason or the measured treatment: a published
                     study whose design isolates the catalyst; a record saying the outcome was passed, created or
                     changed because of it ("in response to", "following", "as a result of", "created to investigate");
                     the catalyst's own institutional aftermath (an inquiry into it, a fund for its victims).
  0.75 contributing  The evidence names the catalyst as one of several causes, or as the push for an effort already
                     under way ("gained popularity following", "partly as a result of", "inspired", "credited with"),
                     or a measured effect that the study says is confined to a subgroup or a channel.
  0.50 contextual    The catalyst is named as context or background, the outcome answers something the catalyst set off
                     at one remove (the trial of the perpetrators, the company's bankruptcy), the outcome is named after
                     the catalyst without a mechanism, or the evidence line names the outcome but not the catalyst and
                     the link rests on a chain the line does not show.
  0.25 incidental    A shared name, an homage, a co-occurrence; the outcome would plausibly have happened in the same
                     form without the catalyst.
  0.00 none          No relation is stated.
  missing            The evidence line is empty and no chain step names the outcome (record as missing, never as 0).
  Myth checks (busted rows): C rates how directly the refutation tests this catalyst's claimed effect (1.00 when the
  study tests that exact claim on that catalyst; 0.75 when it tests a close variant or a general version of it).

G, consequence significance: the mean of three observable subscores. Never use obscurity, novelty or surprise.
  scope        who is bound or covered by the outcome
               1.00 international or multinational (a treaty, several countries); 0.80 national or federal;
               0.55 a state, province or large region; 0.30 a city, a locality, one institution's population;
               0.15 one company, one product or a group of individuals.
  reach        how many people the outcome touches (use the study's own reach field where it exists)
               5 -> 1.00 most adults; 4 -> 0.75 a large share; 3 -> 0.50 a sizable group; 2 -> 0.25 a niche group;
               1 -> 0.00 very niche.
  permanence   institutional permanence or durability
               1.00 a standing law, treaty, institution, or a durable outcome (births, deaths, names, a closure);
               0.70 a multi-year program, policy, fund or trend; 0.40 a change lasting months;
               0.15 a one-off or short effect (days or weeks).
  Myth checks: G rates the outcome the popular story claimed, since a refutation matters in proportion to the claim it
  corrects.
  missing      A subscore that cannot be judged from the fields is recorded as missing; G is then the mean of the
               subscores that exist, and G is missing only when all three are.

Output per candidate: {"id", "C", "C_level", "G_scope", "G_reach", "G_permanence", "G", "reason"}.
