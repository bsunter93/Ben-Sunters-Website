match_v1 (fixed rule for matching a candidate to its catalyst's 25 predictions, for S_A)

Inputs: one candidate's outcome text (and its claim sentence), and the 25 ranked predictions made for its catalyst by
predict_v1. Do not use the evidence line, the panel ratings or any other field.

A prediction MATCHES the candidate when a reader who had written that prediction would say "I expected that":
  specific  The prediction names the same outcome: the same law, body, fund, treaty, product, place or measure, or
            the same change in the same population, in the same direction. Different wording is fine
            ("Congress passes the Oil Pollution Act" matches "The Oil Pollution Act of 1990 is signed").
  class     The prediction names the outcome's kind, its subject and its jurisdiction or population together, at
            a level where the candidate is an instance of it ("a federal law expanding oil spill liability and
            cleanup duties" matches the Oil Pollution Act; "copycat suicides rise in the weeks after" matches
            "more suicides in the US in the following months").

A prediction DOES NOT MATCH when it:
  - names only a kind or a domain ("new laws", "public debate", "media attention", "safety reforms");
  - names a different jurisdiction, body or population (a federal law does not match a state law; a US effect does
    not match an effect measured in another country, unless the prediction says worldwide or names that country);
  - names the opposite direction (more births does not match fewer births);
  - names a step on the way rather than the outcome (attention to a topic does not match a law on it).

Myth checks (busted rows): the candidate's outcome is that a popular claim does not hold up. It is matched only by a
prediction that says the claimed effect was disputed, absent, small or debunked. A prediction that the claimed effect
happened does not match; record its rank separately as claim_predicted_rank, for information.

Record per candidate: the best (lowest) matching rank 1 to 25 or "not predicted", the match level (specific or class),
the matching prediction's text, and a one-line reason. When unsure between class and no match, record no match.

Rank to score (a stated monotone rule, not a probability):
  S_A = 1.0                           if not predicted
  S_A = 0.7 x (rank - 1) / 24         if predicted at rank 1 to 25 (rank 1 -> 0.00, rank 25 -> 0.70)
The gap between rank 25 and "not predicted" marks that missing from the list is a different state from a late rank.
