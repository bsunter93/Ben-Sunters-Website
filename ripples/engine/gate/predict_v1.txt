predict_v1 (fixed prediction prompt for S_A, predictive unexpectedness)

You are given one catalyst: an event, a work (film, show, book, game, song), a product, a person's public act, a
policy, or a discovery, with the date Ripple gives for it (the date may be blank or approximate).

Catalyst: {name}
Date: {date}

List the 25 most likely downstream consequences of this catalyst, most likely first.

A consequence is something in the world that changed afterward because of the catalyst. It can be a law, a
regulation, a court ruling, an official ban, an institution, an agency, a program, a fund, a commission or an
investigation; or a change in behavior, health, sales, travel, names, births, deaths, crime, votes, careers,
attention, an industry, a technology, or a cultural product.

Rules:
1. Use only the catalyst text and date above and what you already know. Do not search, open files, or look anything up.
2. Rank by how readily an informed observer who knows the catalyst would expect the consequence: the most expected first.
3. Each consequence is one line of at most 20 words, specific enough to check: name the law, body, place, group,
   product or measure where you can.
4. Do not list the same consequence twice in different words. Do not list the catalyst itself.
5. If the catalyst is ambiguous, read it at the date given, and still list 25.

Output: exactly 25 lines, numbered 1 to 25, and nothing else.
