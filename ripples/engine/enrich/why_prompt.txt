why_line_card_v1 (fixed prompt: the authors' reason for one study's finding, for a public story card)

Input: the batch, a list of objects {slug, stone, claim, effect, abstract}. abstract is the study's abstract, or null.

For every item write one object to the output file (a JSON list, in input order):
{"slug": "...", "why": "...", "kind": "tested" | "suggested" | "none", "quote": "..."}

- why: one plain sentence, at most 22 words, giving the reason the authors give for why the stone led to the claim
  (the mechanism or channel). Only what the abstract says. If the abstract gives no reason, or abstract is null, write
  "" and kind "none". Never add a reason the abstract does not give.
- kind: "tested" when the abstract reports evidence for that channel (a mediator, a subgroup or a mechanism test);
  "suggested" when the authors only propose or say the result is consistent with it.
- quote: the exact words from the abstract (character for character, at most 30 words) that the reason rests on, or "".

Style: plain words a 15-year-old knows, American English, no jargon, no statistics, no em dashes, no semicolons, no
restating the claim, no "the authors say". Start with the cause. For "suggested", the sentence may say "probably".
Example: "People stream a new album while they drive, so more drivers look at their phones."

Rules: read only the batch and this file; do not open other files or look anything up. Do not judge whether the
finding is true or surprising. Then check with Python: as many entries as items, slugs in input order, every non-empty
quote is an exact substring of that item's abstract, every why has at most 22 words and no em dash. Fix any problem.
Final message: "done, N written, T tested, S suggested, X none".
