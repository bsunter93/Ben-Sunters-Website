study_story_v1 (fixed prompt: the words of one study story, from a verified finding and its paper's text)

Input: a JSON list of items. Each item has slug, stone {name, date}, outcome, direction, effect, design, quote (the
sentence the finding was read from), population_place, paper {title, venue, year}, abstract, and fulltext (a path to
the paper's text, or null when only the abstract was reachable).

For every item write one object to the output file (a JSON list, in input order):
{"slug", "slug_hint", "label", "years", "stone_claim", "stone_date", "date_precision", "date_note", "claim", "short",
 "design", "effect", "effect_short", "effect_quote", "note_quote", "note_more", "range", "skip"}

- slug: copied from the input.
- slug_hint: 2 to 4 lowercase words joined by hyphens, naming the stone and the outcome ("japan-quake-politics").
- label: the stone in at most 5 words, as a reader would name it ("Album release days", "Violent tornadoes").
- years: the study's own window in words: "2005", "2018 to 2022", "1990 to 2017".
- stone_claim: one sentence, at most 18 words, present tense, saying what happened ("A violent tornado hits a US county").
- stone_date: YYYY-MM-DD for the stone, or for the start of the study's window when the stone repeats. When only the
  year is known use YYYY-07-01 and date_precision "year"; when only the month is known use YYYY-MM-15 and "month".
- date_precision: "day", "month" or "year".
- date_note: when the date stands for a window or a repeating event, one sentence: "The study covers ... Dated here to
  ...". Otherwise "".
- claim: the finding as a headline, at most 12 words, past tense, plain ("More people joined political groups").
  Say what the paper found and no more: keep its population and its comparison when they matter.
- short: the finding in at most 4 words, sentence case, no period ("More political groups").
- design: what the study compared, in plain words, at most 20 words ("counties hit by a tornado against similar
  counties that were not").
- effect: one sentence, at most 30 words, giving the paper's own numbers for this finding. If the texts give no number
  for it, write "".
- effect_short: the lead number as it should appear large, at most 12 characters ("+15%", "2.7% fewer", "+10 points"),
  or "" when effect is "".
- effect_quote: ONE contiguous passage from the abstract or the fulltext, at most 60 words, copied as written, that
  holds every number in claim, effect and effect_short. You may join a broken line with one space and rejoin a word
  split by a hyphen at a line end. "" when effect is "".
- note_quote: one sentence from the abstract, copied exactly, that states the finding (at most 40 words).
- note_more: one or two plain sentences on who and where the study covers, from the abstract only, with no number the
  abstract does not give. "" if the abstract does not say.
- range: [start, end] as YYYY-MM-DD, the study's window.
- skip: "" normally. When the texts do not state this finding, or state it for a different group or outcome than the
  item names, write the reason in a few words and leave the other fields as "".

Numbers: copy them from the passage. Do not compute, convert or average. You may round in claim only ("about 18" for
18.2). A percent stays a percent; points stay points; a standard deviation is not a percent.

Never give a reason or mechanism for the finding (another step does that, from the authors' words). Never add a fact
the texts do not give. Style: plain words a 15-year-old knows, American English, no jargon, no statistics words (no
"significant", "coefficient", "estimate"), no em dashes, no semicolons, no "the authors say".

Rules: read only the batch, this file and the fulltext files the batch names. The fulltext files are long: search them
with Python for the outcome's words and numbers rather than reading them whole. Do not look anything up. Then check
with Python: as many entries as items, slugs in input order; every effect_quote and note_quote, after collapsing all
whitespace to single spaces, is a substring of that item's collapsed abstract or fulltext; every number in claim,
effect and effect_short appears in effect_quote (claim may round); lengths as above; no em dash anywhere. Fix any
failure. Final message: "done, N written, S skipped".
