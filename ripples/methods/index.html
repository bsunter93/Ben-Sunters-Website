Methods and receipts

The Ripple Map looks for hidden knock-on effects: an upstream shock, then a public series somewhere else in life that moved against its own normal afterwards. This page says exactly how a stop earns its tier, prints every number we test ourselves against, and keeps the misses.

Method 6.0. Numbers on the map come only from SQL over public data. Every published version is frozen and chained in a public ledger.

What a stop claims, and what it never claims

A stop says four things: when a series moved, how far it moved against its own weekday and seasonal normal, how rare a move like that is against placebo tests, and how many independent sources agree. It names the mechanism that proposed the link and says where that mechanism came from.

It never claims cause. The words on every card are "consistent with, never proof of cause". We never give a share or an amount attributable to the shock, a dollar or job figure, a chance that a whole chain is real, or anything about stocks or trades. A route from an input-output table is a hypothesis, drawn as grey italic text, never as a stop.

The tiers

TierWhat has to hold
Measured At its pre-registered final look: false-discovery-adjusted q ≤ 0.05; at least one real-world outcome channel moved (not just attention); two independent channels agree, or one outcome channel plus an independent link; it moved after its parent, not before; no market-wide or holiday shock that day; no pre-trend; the shock beats rival events in the same week; it holds when any one source is dropped; every source is past its warm-up; the parent is the shock itself or a Measured stop; and its cell is not circuit-broken.
Likely q ≤ 0.20 at any look; or q ≤ 0.05 with exactly one Measured condition failing, which the card names ("attention ripple", "source still warming up", "already moving"…); or q ≤ 0.01 at an interim look, shown as provisional.
Watching Registered before we looked, with a known mechanism and its window still open. It shows the due date and the pre-registered base rate. When the window closes it becomes a stop or a grey stub. Nothing is deleted.
Stayed flat ⊥Window closed, never reached Likely. Counted in every denominator and drawn as a grey stub at its fork.
RetractedWas Likely or Measured, then revised data, a later rival event, a recalibration or the circuit breaker pushed it below the bar. It stays on the line, struck through, with the date and reason.

An attention-only co-move (readers moving together) is capped at Likely and labelled "attention ripple". Tiers are never multiplied along a line; depth-two stops read "if the previous step holds".

How one stop is tested

  • Frozen first. Every morning at 07:34 UTC each shock's candidate paths, their prior weights and the day's test family are written and hashed into the ledger before any post-onset number is read.
  • Its own normal. The baseline is 91 days ending three weeks before the shock. Daily series are matched by weekday, holidays are modelled, and series with more than 400 days of history get a year-ago term. Weekly and monthly series are compared with their own 36-month history at each release.
  • A fixed statistic per channel. Peak channels (attention, prediction markets, daily prices) use the largest move in the window; cumulative channels (builders, real world, institutions) use the summed move, corrected for autocorrelation; slow channels use the first release that covers the window. Nothing is chosen per stop.
  • After, not before. The onset must come on or after the parent's onset. A series that was already climbing in the four weeks before is flagged and capped at Likely.
  • Independent sources. Channels combine with weights and their measured correlation; the channel that proposed the link (for example, news co-mentions) is never counted as agreement.
  • The size shown is shrunk. The multiple on the card is pulled toward 1× to undo the winner's curse and carries an 80% interval. The raw peak is in "Show the math".

Could it be luck? Two numbers, two labels

Lookalikes. "A random pairing looks this strong about 1 in N times." Each stop is re-run with identical code on fake dates (at least 200, always including the same days last year), fake starts (other events the same week) and fake pages (series of the same type). N comes from the family that makes the stop look least rare.

Fluke rate. "Links this strong from decoy starts turn out to be flukes about 1 in K times." Every day eight calm pages that weren't trending go through the same candidate generation, tests and looks. Anything they pass is false by construction; K compares their pass rate with the real one, over the last 90 days, per strength band. Until enough decoys exist the card says "warming up" and Measured needs an extra-strict test.

Neither number is the probability that a stop is false. On top of both, one weighted Benjamini–Hochberg procedure runs over every final look each day, with decoys inside the same run, and every page prints its denominators: paths tested, how many moved, how many are Measured, and how many false links to expect. The home page shows the control ripple beside the real lines every day.

Looks, retractions and ageing

Attention channels are looked at daily; real-world travel at +3 and +7 days; builders at +7 and +14; weekly jobless claims at each release to +28; monthly series at each release to +90. An interim look can grant at most Likely (provisional). Measured is granted only at the final look. Once a stop is published as Likely or better, its attention evidence is frozen, so our own publicity can't feed it.

A circuit breaker watches each path-type and channel cell. If decoys pass too often for 14 days, every Measured stop in that cell drops to Likely and a banner says so here.

v1 post-mortem: 0 of 120

An earlier version of this project published 120 cross-signal "discoveries". Re-run with a corrected out-of-sample test and placebo controls, 0 of 120 survived. Randomly paired signals look like noise; hypothesis-driven pairs did better, but the headline hits came from testing many pairs without controls.

The Ripple Map is built around that lesson: candidate sets frozen before testing, placebo dates on every stop, decoy starts to measure the false-pass rate, one false-discovery procedure over everything, positive and negative controls, published denominators, and a hash-chained ledger.

Privacy

We keep no accounts and no play history. If you tap Share or arrive from a shared link, we count it: a daily count per source, with a random browser ID hashed under a salt we delete each day. No IP address, no page history. Waitlist emails are stored only for that list and can't be read back through the site. Your theme, last visit and private hunches stay in your browser.
