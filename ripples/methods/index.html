Knock-On play

Measured attention, not proof of cause.

Methods and track record

Knock-On is a daily game built on a measurement engine called Today's Ripples. Every right answer is a page whose Wikipedia readers spiked after its parent. Every wrong answer is a real linked page that stayed flat. This page says exactly how that is decided, and prints the misses.

Method version 5.0. The numbers on the game come only from SQL over public data. AI-written captions are labelled and can never add hops, numbers or options.

What gets measured

  • Views. Daily Wikipedia pageviews, agent=user, all access methods, on a log scale: x = ln(1 + views).
  • Baseline. For a reference day τ, the 91 days from τ−111 to τ−21. m = median of x; s = max(0.1, 1.4826 × MAD); z = (x − m) / s. "Normal readers" means em − 1 a day. The seed card shades this window on its sparkline.
  • Seed. A page that appeared in a seed source (Wikipedia most-read lists, Google Trends daily RSS) in the last 3 days, resolves to an English Wikipedia article, has an onset (first day in the last 10 with z ≥ 3, in a run that reaches z ≥ 5), a peak of at least 3× normal, passes the safety filter, was not a puzzle seed in the last 30 days, and is not only a Main Page feature.
  • Biggest day in N days. N counts back to the most recent earlier day with at least as many views as the peak. With no such day, it says "since records began" (July 2015).

How a hop is tested

Candidates are fixed per parent before any testing: the parent's top 20 clickstream destinations for the latest month, then its live outlinks with a 60-day median of at least 300 views a day, capped at 60. Disambiguation, list, date and year pages, the parent itself and blocklisted pages are excluded.

TestRule
SpikeS = the highest z in the 4 days from the parent's onset. Passes at S ≥ 3 and a peak of at least 1.5× normal.
TimingThe candidate's own onset is the first day in [τp−7, τp+3] with z ≥ 3. Earlier than the parent: rejected. Same day: "alongside". Later: "spiked after".
Time-shift placeboThe same test repeated at fake onsets 3, 4, … about 38 weeks earlier. p = (1 + weeks that did as well) / (1 + weeks tested), at best about 0.026.
Fluke meterEvery day, 8 calm pages (|z| < 1 for 14 days), matched to the real seeds on size and topic, are given a seed's onset and expanded with identical code. Anything they pass is a fluke by construction. Per strength band of S, the fluke rate f = (decoy pass rate) / (real pass rate) over the last 90 days, shown as "about 1 in K".
Shown hopSpike and timing pass, p ≤ 0.10 and f ≤ 0.20.
Answer hopp ≤ 0.05 and f ≤ 0.10; up at least 1.3× on both desktop and mobile; not a Main Page feature within a day of its onset; and at depth 2 or more, no shared-trigger flag (it is not in the seed's own candidate set and is not an earlier-onset hop of another of today's seeds). A flagged hop stops the chain.

While the decoy pool has fewer than 500 tests, the meter shows "warming up" and an answer hop needs p ≤ 0.05 anyway. The meter never combines odds across a chain.

Decoys: the wrong answers

Each round's three wrong options are real pages linked from the same parent that stayed calm: highest |z| under 1 and a peak under 1.25× normal since a week before the parent's onset, a normal readership within 0.5–2× the answer's, a different title stem, and at least two of the three in the answer's topic. If there aren't three calm siblings, that hop can't be a round. After you answer, you see their flat sparklines next to the spike.

Evidence labels

  • 📈 Spiked after / alongside the timing test above.
  • 👣 Readers clicked through only when Wikipedia's monthly clickstream has the exact parent → answer link in its top 20 for the latest month. It is the only label that talks about readers moving between pages.
  • 🔗 Linked from the parent's article links to it.
  • 📱💻 Desktop and mobile the rise shows on both.
  • 🎲 Fluke meter as above, or "warming up".
  • Also on other channels corroboration badges from other public sources. They are not part of the measurement and never change a result.
  • Players' picks what other players chose, labelled players' picks and shown at 30 or more players. Crowd numbers are never evidence.
  • AI-written context an optional caption by Claude, hedged and about attention only.

Chains, rounds and Call It

Chains are ranked by confidence first: the number of answer hops, then the summed log fluke odds; topic jumps only break ties. A chain of 3+ hops becomes the puzzle (capped at 4). A 2-hop chain is topped up with "fresh ripples", single hops from other seeds, shown as a · break. With fewer than 3 rounds the puzzle comes from the reserve bank of the last 14 days and shows its real date. With nothing at all, the page says it is delayed. There is no padding: the chain ends where the evidence ends.

Call It offers 4 calm pages linked from round 1's seed, each with a median of at least 2,000 views a day, so this site's own traffic can't move them. A call hits when any day in the 7-day window reaches z ≥ 3 against a baseline frozen on the puzzle date and at least 1.5× normal. The Model v0 (base rate) is the historical hit rate of such pages, adjusted by clickstream rank when that data exists. Crowd and Model are shown as raw counts until 200 calls resolve; Brier scores and skill come after that, printed even if skill ≤ 0.

The Board

Splash is a seed's peak multiple. Wake is how many of a fixed set of 20 linked pages (clickstream top 20, else top outlinks) passed the shown-hop test. Big Wave: splash ≥ 10 and wake ≥ 3. Sleeper: splash under 10, wake ≥ 3. Belly Flop: splash ≥ 10, wake 0, at least 15 tested. Ripple: everything else. Natural disasters appear without a label or nickname.

Safety

Excluded from seeds, options and Call It: people who died in the last 60 days; violence, crime events, terrorism, war and battles, mass-casualty disasters, suicide, sexual or adult topics and hate; titles like "Death of", "Killing of", "Shooting of", massacres, attacks and bombings; living private individuals; and a manual blocklist. An optional daily AI review can veto a puzzle before 07:20 UTC, never add to it.

The ledger

Each published puzzle is hashed (puzzle, reveal and Call It odds, without captions) and chained to the previous row, so nobody, including us, can quietly change a past answer. The chain is public at ledger.json; the current head is in the track record above.

v1 post-mortem: 0 of 120

An earlier version of this project published 120 cross-signal "discoveries". Re-run with a corrected out-of-sample test and placebo controls, 0 of 120 survived. Randomly paired signals look like noise; hypothesis-driven pairs did better, but the headline hits came from testing many pairs without controls. v5 is built around that lesson: candidate sets fixed before testing, time-shift placebos on every hop, decoy starting pages to measure the false-pass rate, a stop rule for shared triggers, a monthly re-grade against real clickstream, and a hash-chained ledger.

Privacy

We store a hashed random ID, a daily-salted hash of your IP (for rate limits; the salt is deleted after the day), your picks and your country. No accounts. Waitlist emails are stored only for that list and cannot be read back through the site. Streaks, history and calls live in your browser's local storage.
